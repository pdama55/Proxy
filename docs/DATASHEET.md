# Datasheet: Proxy — reporting fidelity in delegated LLM negotiation

Following Gebru et al., *Datasheets for Datasets*. This describes the released episode corpus, its scores and
its human annotations. The preregistration is `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md` (OSF: https://osf.io/hb3f2); departures
from it are in `docs/DEVIATIONS.md`.

## Motivation

**Why was it created?** When an LLM agent negotiates on someone's behalf, that person usually learns what
happened only from the agent's own report. The corpus was built to measure whether those reports convey what
the principal's own instructions cost them — specifically, whether a report mentions a better deal the
counterparty offered that only the principal's floor requirement ruled out, and whether it states the deal's
value correctly.

**Who created it and who funded it?** Parth Dama (independent researcher, ORCID 0009-0008-8452-3680). No
sponsor, grant, or model developer funded, reviewed, or had sight of the work; all API costs were paid
personally.

## Composition

**What do instances represent?** One instance is a negotiation *episode*: a full bilateral negotiation between
an LLM agent and a scripted counterparty, the agent's written report to its client, and every score computed
over them. Each episode record contains the scenario and its exact utilities, the briefing given to the agent,
every turn with the package offered, the termination state, the report text, mechanical detector output, judge
scores, a knowledge-probe exchange, and a simulated principal's ratings.

**How many instances?** 9,000+ episodes across all runs, including pilots and arms not used in the paper. The
preregistered confirmatory grid is 2,880. Other arms: a five-model Qwen ladder (480), two breadth runs of 100
fresh scenarios each (400), two report-intervention arms re-asking the confirmatory negotiations under
different prompts (2,400 each), a post-freeze Claude Fable 5.1 arm (240), phrasing and salience ablations
(432), and an LLM-counterparty arm (96).

**Is it a sample or complete?** Complete for the configuration space each run defines: every cell of the
model × outcome-target × audit-framing × role grid is generated once, with no optional stopping and no
post-hoc selection. It is not a sample of naturally occurring negotiations, and does not claim to be.

**What data does each instance contain?** Generated text (agent turns, agent reports, judge and probe
responses), structured state (packages, utilities, points), and derived scores. Scenarios are synthetic,
produced from integer seeds by `proxy/env/scenario.py`. There are no real firms, people, prices or contracts.

**Is any information missing?** Judge and probe scores are absent for episodes that errored or that a scoring
pass could not complete; these are reported per model. Some measures apply only conditionally by construction
— the blocked-alternative measure only where such an offer existed, the stated-total measure only where a
report states a total. The paper reports unconditional estimands and worst-case bounds alongside the
conditional rates for this reason.

**Does it contain confidential or personal data?** No. No human subject data, no personal data, no
user-generated content. The "confidential figure" inside scenarios is a synthetic reservation value planted
for the leak measure.

**Could it be offensive or distressing?** The domain is commercial procurement. Reports can be inaccurate or
misleading about deal terms, which is the object of study, but the content is not otherwise sensitive.

## Collection process

**How was the data collected?** Generated by the harness in this repository against hosted model APIs:
Anthropic's direct API, Microsoft Azure AI Foundry, and OpenRouter. No model was run locally; no training or
fine-tuning was performed.

**Over what timeframe?** Confirmatory episodes were generated 2026-09-15 to 2026-09-16 UTC, deliberately
within a short window to limit provider-side model drift. Supplementary arms ran through 2026-09-17. The
provider's served-model string is recorded on every episode; those strings, not product names, identify what
actually answered. No served-model string changed mid-arm.

**Sampling strategy.** Scenario seeds are fixed integers, listed per config. The design is paired: every model
and condition sees the same scenarios, constraints, first mover and counterparty messages. One replicate per
cell. Decoding uses provider defaults, with no temperature or seed set, so individual episodes are not
reproducible token-for-token; the unit of replication is the scenario, and all uncertainty is quantified by
resampling scenarios.

**Who was involved?** The author, plus one unpaid volunteer annotator who consented to the use of their
labels. Neither annotator interacted with any model; both read stored transcripts after the fact.

**Ethical review.** None sought. On the standard criteria — no human subjects, no personal data — none was
required.

## Preprocessing, cleaning, labeling

**What preprocessing was done?** Mechanical detectors (`proxy/scoring/detectors.py`, `detectors-v4`) compute
exposure, disclosure lower bounds, stated totals and leak flags from raw text. Model judges score disclosure
and portrayal; a separate model simulates a principal reading the report. The harness enforces that no model
judges its own output and that judges come from families with no model under test.

**Is raw data retained?** Yes. Every episode stores full turn-by-turn text and the verbatim report, so any
detector or judge can be recomputed from source. `proxy analyze all configs/analysis.yaml` regenerates every
number reported in the paper.

**Human labels.** Two annotators independently labelled a stratified sample — 50 disclosure items and 25
stated-total items — blind to model, condition, detector output and judge output, under guidelines fixed in
advance (`docs/annotation_guidelines.md`, hash recorded in each batch manifest). Per-annotator labels are
released un-aggregated, not merely their consensus. Portrayal ratings and leak detection received **no** human
validation and are reported as secondary throughout.

## Uses

**What has it been used for?** The accompanying paper.

**What else could it be used for?** Studying agent self-reporting, summarization fidelity against ground
truth, LLM negotiation behaviour, judge calibration, and principal-agent information asymmetry in delegated
systems. The corpus is unusual in pairing a full negotiation transcript with exact ground-truth utilities and
the agent's own account of it, which makes report fidelity checkable rather than a matter of opinion.

**What should it not be used for?** It should not be used to estimate how often such trade-offs arise in real
delegated work. The environment creates the trade-off deliberately and at a fixed point in every negotiation,
so exposure is near-universal by design. Rates here answer "when a blocked better deal exists, how often is it
conveyed" — not "how often does this situation occur." It should also not be used to rank models on
negotiating skill, which the scripted counterparty bounds by construction.

**Risks and harms.** A specification of what agents omit is also a specification of how to produce omissions.
The mitigating facts: the behaviour is what models already do by default, unprompted, so the corpus reveals
nothing a reader could not obtain by running an agent themselves; and it ships with a defence, since the
intervention arms identify a prompt that moves non-disclosure from 97% to 27%.

## Maintenance

**Who maintains it?** The author (parth55dama@gmail.com).

**Will it be updated?** The confirmatory corpus is frozen; corrections, if any, will be recorded in
`docs/DEVIATIONS.md` and released as new versions rather than silent edits. The deviations log is the
authoritative record of every change made after the plan was frozen.

**How can others contribute or extend it?** The harness is released in full. Adding a model requires a config
entry; adding a condition requires a config change and a new run name. Scenario generation is deterministic
from seeds, so an extension can reuse the exact scenarios used here.

## Known limitations

- Provider-default decoding and one replicate per cell: within-cell sampling variance is not estimated.
- Several models are served behind undated aliases rather than pinned snapshots, so exact reproduction cannot
  be guaranteed even against the recorded string.
- The simulated principal is unvalidated against human readers.
- Human validation is thinner than preregistered (50 and 25 items against 150/150/100/50 planned), and
  characterization and leak measures have none.
- Leak detection is verbal only; a confidential figure could leak through a concession pattern uncounted.
