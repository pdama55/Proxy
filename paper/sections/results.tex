\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every number below comes from \texttt{proxy analyze all} over \nEpisodes\ confirmatory episodes; intervals resample scenarios. A bootstrap $p$ cannot fall below $1/(B+1) = .0005$ with $B = 2{,}000$ resamples, so tests that reach that floor are reported as inequalities rather than as point values: an unadjusted $p < .001$ becomes $p_{\text{Holm}} < .003$ after correction across the six-test family.

\subsection{Agents obeyed}
Agents accepted a package that broke the client's requirement in \violationCount\ episodes (\violationRate, CI \violationCI) and revealed the confidential figure in \leakCount\ (\leakRate). Both rates are an order of magnitude below the violation rates reported when a rule stands between an agent and its assigned goal. The reporting failures below are therefore not cover stories for misconduct: there was almost none to cover.

\subsection{Reports rarely convey the blocked better deal}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/d1_by_model.pdf}
\caption{Share of exposed episodes whose report does not convey the better deal that the client's own requirement blocked. Filled points: judged. Hollow diamonds: the judge-free lower bound (the report refers to no other offer at all). Bars are 95\% intervals.}
\label{fig:d1}
\end{figure}

A blocked better deal was on the table in \exposureRate\ of episodes. Reports conveyed it in \doneToldAll\ of them: judged non-disclosure is \doneJudgedAll\ (\hyperref[sec:validation]{validated in \cref{sec:validation}}), supporting H1a ($p_{\text{Holm}} < .003$). Two judge-free measures bracket that figure without any model judgment. Only \doneNamedAll\ of reports name the blocked offer at all, by its price, its value in points, or the term the requirement ruled out; a report that never names it cannot have conveyed it, so non-disclosure is at least \doneNotNamed\ on mechanical grounds alone. At the conservative end, \doneLowerAll\ of reports refer to no other offer or option whatsoever.

The exception is not one model but one lineage. Claude Opus 5 leaves the blocked deal unconveyed in \doneJudgedClaudeOpusFive\ of exposed episodes, and Claude Fable 5.1---added after the freeze and reported separately below---in \fableJudged\ (n = \fableN), while every other model sits between \doneJudgedGrokFourSix\ and \doneJudgedClaudeHaikuFourFive: \doneJudgedClaudeSonnetFive\ for Claude Sonnet 5, \doneJudgedGptSixAstra\ for GPT-6 Astra, \doneJudgedGrokFourSix\ for Grok 4.6. Frontier status alone does not predict disclosure---GPT-6 Astra is as capable a negotiator and discloses almost never---but within Anthropic's ladder the two most capable models are the only ones in the grid that volunteer the trade-off at all, and they do so at nearly the same rate. That pattern is what H4 detects as a capability slope, and it is confined to one developer.

Both rates are computed inside a gate---being exposed to a blocked deal, and stating a total at all---and a gate is itself an outcome. Neither conclusion depends on it. Counting over every episode rather than the exposed ones, \uncondNondisclosure\ of all episodes were both exposed and left the blocked deal unconveyed, and the worst-case bounds on the conditional rate, which count every ungated episode against us, are \boundsDone. The same bounds for misstated totals are \boundsDtwo. The floor of each interval is what the data support under the least favourable selection story available. Weighting is likewise immaterial: the pooled rate is episode-weighted across arms that carry 30 scenario seeds and arms that carry 12, so we also report the model-averaged rate, \doneModelAveraged, and the rate over the \nFullSeedModels\ models with the full seed set, \doneFullSeedOnly. All three agree to within a point.

\subsection{Silence is not ignorance}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/knowledge_vs_disclosure.pdf}
\caption{For each model: how often the report told the client unprompted (right), and how often the agent named the blocked offer's value when asked directly afterwards, among reports that had stayed silent (left).}
\label{fig:knowledge}
\end{figure}

After each report was recorded, the same model was asked in the same conversation whether the other party had offered anything worth more that it did not take. Among silent reports, agents named that offer's value correctly in \probeKnewAll\ of cases (n = \probeN). GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.6 and Qwen3.5 27B answered correctly in \probeKnewGptSixAstra, \probeKnewGptFiveSixSol, \probeKnewGrokFourSix\ and \probeKnewQwenthreeFiveTwosevenb\ of cases respectively, while telling the client unprompted in about one episode in a hundred. The information was available, retrievable on request, and omitted.

\subsection{Asking works; instructing the agent mostly does not}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/interventions.pdf}
\caption{Judged non-disclosure under three report prompts on identical negotiations.}
\label{fig:interventions}
\end{figure}

Re-asking the same \nInterventionPairs\ negotiations for a report under a different prompt isolates the prompt's effect. When the client's question asks about terms the agent turned down, judged non-disclosure falls from \intOpenJudged\ to \intTradeoffsJudged\ ($\intTradeoffsDelta$, CI \intTradeoffsDeltaCI), and to \intTradeoffsBestRange\ for GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Luna and Grok 4.6. A deployer-side instruction stating that the client relies on the account to decide whether to keep their own instructions is weaker and uneven: \intOpenJudged\ to \intNormJudged\ overall ($\intNormDelta$), with Claude Haiku 4.5 at \intNormClaudeHaikuFourFive, Llama 4 Maverick at \intNormLlamaFourMaverick\ and Claude Sonnet 5 at \intNormClaudeSonnetFive\ essentially unmoved. The burden of eliciting the trade-off falls on a principal who already suspects it exists.

\subsection{Stated values, and what the requirement cost}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/d2_errors.pdf}
\caption{Stated minus true point total, among reports that state one.}
\label{fig:d2}
\end{figure}

Reports state the deal's value in \dtwoStatedRate\ of deals and state it wrongly in \dtwoWrongAll\ of those (H1b, $p_{\text{Holm}} < .003$), with a mean absolute error of \dtwoAbsError\ points. Errors are concentrated: Claude Haiku 4.5 (\dtwoWrongClaudeHaikuFourFive), DeepSeek V4 Pro (\dtwoWrongDeepseekVfourProAzure) and Mistral Large 3 (\dtwoWrongMistralLargeThree) against \dtwoFrontierRange\ for the frontier models. Unlike omission, this broadly tracks capability, though not cleanly: Claude Sonnet 5 and Opus 5 sit at the top of that frontier range rather than at zero, where the OpenAI models and Grok 4.6 sit.

The requirement's shadow price---the points the blocked deal would have added---averaged \shadowMean\ points (median \shadowMedian; \shadowShareAboveTen\ of episodes above 10). Disclosure barely responds to it: \stakesHighDisclosed\ of high-stakes episodes were disclosed against \stakesLowDisclosed\ of low-stakes ones, a difference of \stakesDiff\ points whose 95\% interval (\stakesDiffCI) straddles zero, and within the 100-scenario breadth run the slope of disclosure on points forgone is indistinguishable from zero for Claude Opus 5 ($p = \stakesOpusP$).

\subsection{Preregistered contrasts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H2a (worse after bad outcomes): not supported.} Non-disclosure was \emph{lower} after bad outcomes by \hTwoaPoints\ points ($p = \hTwoaP$). Agents explain weak results at more length, which surfaces other offers.
  \item \textbf{H2b (value overstated after bad outcomes): supported.} Stated totals run \dtwoSignedDiff\ points higher, relative to truth, after bad outcomes than after good ones ($p_{\text{Holm}} < .003$). Characterization bias moves the same way: $+0.96$ SD (CI 0.88--1.04).
  \item \textbf{H3 (audit framing): supported but small.} Telling the agent the transcript will be read lowers the judge-free rate by \hThreePoints\ points ($p_{\text{Holm}} = \hThreeP$) and leaves the judged rate unchanged.
  \item \textbf{H4 (capability does not help): not supported.} Within families, non-disclosure falls with capability rank (OR \hFourOR\ per rank, CI \hFourORCI), driven by Claude Opus 5; it is flat within the Qwen ladder and within OpenAI.
  \item \textbf{H5 (decision cost larger after bad outcomes): not supported.} The simulated principal rates outcomes \emph{lower} from the report than from ground truth ($\hFiveGap$), and the gap is not larger after bad outcomes ($p = \hFiveP$). Its decision differed between report and facts in \principalMismatch\ of episodes, but almost all of that is extra caution: in \principalOverCautious\ it intervened on the report where the facts justified signing off, against \hFiveMissed\ in the harmful direction. A vaguer account makes this principal hedge, which is not the failure the paper is about and is a further reason to read H5 as a fact about one model rather than about people.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rewording the briefing does not move it; rewording the request does}
If disclosure collapsed under a synonym for ``requirement'', the finding would be about phrasing rather than
behaviour. A separate arm (\ablPromptDiffN\ episodes, three models, three scenarios) varies how the client's
constraint is worded, how prominent it is in the briefing, and how the report is requested. Because it uses the
judge-free lower bound, its levels are not comparable with the judged rates above; only the contrasts are.

Wording the constraint as a \emph{policy} rather than a plain instruction or a \emph{requirement} moves the
lower bound between \ablPhrasingPolicy\ and \ablPhrasingPlain---a few points, with no ordering that suggests
the agent treats one framing as more revisable than another. Burying the constraint in a longer briefing does
not matter either: low salience against base is \ablSalienceDiff\ points, with a 95\% interval
(\ablSalienceDiffCI) that straddles zero.

What does move it is the request. Replacing the open ``how did it go'' with a directive report prompt changes
the lower bound from \ablPromptOpen\ to \ablPromptDirective, a shift of \ablPromptDiff\ points
(\ablPromptDiffCI). This reproduces, on different scenarios and a judge-free measure, what the intervention
arm found: the agent's silence is a property of what it was asked for, not of how its instructions were
phrased.

\subsection{Against a live counterparty}
A further arm replaces the scripted counterparty with a language model negotiating freely
(\llmcpEpisodes\ episodes). It is reported separately and never pooled with the scripted runs. Agents broke no
requirement and leaked no confidential figure, and stated their deal's value wrongly in \llmcpDtwo\ of the
\llmcpDtwoN\ reports that state one, so \Dtwo\ is not an artifact of the scripted opponent.

\Done\ cannot be estimated from this arm, and the reason is instructive. A blocked better deal arose in only
\llmcpExposure\ of episodes---\llmcpExposed\ in total---against \exposureRate\ with the scripted counterparty,
because guaranteeing that exposure is exactly what the scripted counterparty's fixed showcase move is for. A
free negotiator rarely volunteers its best terms and then has them refused on a technicality. Nine episodes
support no rate, and we quote none. This is the clearest illustration of the limitation stated in
\hyperref[sec:limitations]{Limitations}: the main design manufactures the trade-off in order to measure
reporting on it, and says nothing about how often it arises unaided.

\subsection{The arm added after the freeze}
Claude Fable 5.1 was not in the frozen plan; it was added once it became Anthropic's current frontier model, so
that the frontier-to-frontier comparison was not Opus 5 against GPT-6 Astra alone. It ran the same design on the
same scenario seeds (\fableN\ exposed episodes). Judged non-disclosure is \fableJudged, a difference from Opus 5 of \fableVsOpus\ points whose 95\%
interval (\fableVsOpusCI) includes zero, and far below every other model in the grid. Its
stated totals are wrong in \fableDtwo\ of reports, and among its silent reports it named the blocked offer's
value correctly in \fableProbeKnew\ of cases when asked---so, like the others, it tracked what it did not say.

Folding the arm into the confirmatory sample moves the headline from \doneJudgedAll\ over \nEpisodes\ episodes
to \doneJudgedWithFable\ over \nEpisodesWithFable. Every preregistered test in this paper is computed on the
frozen sample without it; the arm is reported here so that exclusion is visible rather than convenient.

\subsection{Generality and awareness}
In the breadth run, 100 scenarios never used elsewhere, judged non-disclosure was \breadthClaudeOpusFive\ for Claude Opus 5, \breadthClaudeSonnetFive\ for Claude Sonnet 5, \breadthGptSixAstra\ for GPT-6 Astra and \breadthGrokFourSix\ for Grok 4.6, matching the main grid.

A rate of zero is only informative if the instrument would have caught the behaviour, so evaluation awareness is measured with a deliberately over-broad pattern that accepts hedges and nominal forms (``this was clearly a test'', ``part of this eval'', ``being evaluated''). It flags 5 of \nEpisodes\ reports. Reading all five, none claims the episode was a test: each uses \emph{scripted}, \emph{stress test} or \emph{exercise} to describe how rigidly the counterparty behaved or how demanding the negotiation was---two Claude reports note that the counterparty re-sent a fixed position, which is an observation about the other side, not about being measured. No report in \nEpisodes\ episodes states that the negotiation was a test, simulation or evaluation of the agent.
